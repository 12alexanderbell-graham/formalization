## 1. SELECTED — Cosmography: low-redshift expansions in FLRW (lookback time, χ, d_L, d_A)
- **Importance:** the Hubble series (Visser 2004/2005; Weinberg 1972 ch. 14) is how supernova and distance-ladder data are analysed model-independently (cosmography: q₀, j₀, s₀ fits). Taught in every cosmology course.
- **Checkability:** pure real analysis on the scale factor a(t): Taylor/Peano, local inversion, little-o algebra. All in Mathlib. Physlib already has the FLRW structure, `redshift`, `comovingDistance`, `transverseComovingDistance`, `decelerationParameter` (Distances.lean, Basic.lean), so results plug in upstream.
- **Error potential (the reason it outranks plain formalization):** published cosmographic series go to z⁴–z⁵ with jerk, snap, lerk and curvature terms and are copied between papers; higher-order coefficients and their stated validity (the z-series diverges for z > 1 in common models, Cattoen–Visser 2007) are where mistakes and hidden assumptions live. Plan: Lean-certified coefficients, then screen the published tables against them (sympy screen first, Lean for confirmation). Hidden-assumption angle already visible: how many derivatives of a(t) each order really needs, and whether "+ …" is asymptotic or convergent.
- **Prior art:** Physlib TODOs "Define the lookback time … prove its model-independent expansion" and "Prove the low-redshift expansions of χ, d_L and d_A … in terms of q₀" are open (Distances.lean). Cosmology in Physlib is actively developed by one contributor (P. Kevorkian, PRs #1634–#1861, Oct 2026): collision risk, mitigated by staying Mathlib-only until a contribution is prepared. No Lean/Isabelle/Coq cosmography found in a web search (2026-10-30).
- **First milestone:** lookback time to second order, o(z²) remainder. **DONE: PROVED this run** (see STATE).
- **Who it helps:** Physlib (closes two TODOs), cosmographers (machine-checked coefficients), anyone teaching the Hubble law.

## THE RESULT (Alexander 2026-10-31; PROMPT standing instruction 10): the goal itself, published

**Claim to establish:** AI can do scientific work whose output a scientist can trust without checking it, because every link from the claim to its meaning is machine-checked, and the system's rate of accepting wrong science is measured and is zero on everything thrown at it.

**What the demonstration consists of (all pursued, none optional):**
1. **Every link machine-checked.** Definitions (T1, done and CI-enforced), statements (T2: non-vacuity done; next hypothesis necessity, quantifier/junk-value mutation), transcriptions (T3: 36/38 dual-read; next reproduction of printed numbers by machine), numbers (T4: certified enclosures in Lean for every numeric claim), simulations (rigorous simulators: validated ODE/PDE integration with proved error bounds), data claims (test suites: a claim compiled into an executable, certified check against the data). Wherever a link still rests on a model's judgment, replace the model with a mechanical cross-check (CAS, closed-form anchors, a second formulation proved equivalent), not with another model.

3. **Measured trust.** False-acceptance rate (T5): large adversarial sets of plausible wrong claims of every kind (wrong definition, vacuous statement, misread formula, wrong number, wrong simulation, wrong data claim), seeded by red teams of different models and by mechanical mutation; the rate with and without each check.
4. **Validated once against outside experts.** A blind comparison in which independent scientists check a sample of certified outputs; the system must have missed nothing they find. ### B1 — Is the high-redshift "tension with ΛCDM" from cosmographic Hubble diagrams an artifact of the truncated expansion? (ACTIVE alongside target 1)
- **Status (run 0828Z): two-point separation and direction PROVED in Work** (`Work/Cosmography/B1Separation.lean`: a cubic agreeing with exact ΛCDM within 0.05 mag at z = 3, 7 lies > 5/4 from the ΛCDM point, on the side a₂ − a₂^ΛCDM < 7 log₁₀2 (a₃ − a₃^ΛCDM)); referee: the RL19 offset points the other way (candidate C27). Next: fit-level version over z ≤ 5.1.
- **Status (run 0428Z): first milestone PROVED + AUDITED** (`Checked/Cosmography/B1Truncation.lean`): for every Ω_m ∈ [0.2, 0.4], the ΛCDM-implied cubic misses exact ΛCDM by > 0.09 mag at z = 3, > 0.3 mag at z = 7, shape > 0.18 mag (normalisation-free). Next: separation theorem (any cubic matching ΛCDM within ε over the data range lies ≥ δ from the ΛCDM point), then comparison with the published contours (`runs/2026-10-31T0428Z/rl19_numbers.md`).
- **Exact problem:** Risaliti & Lusso 2019 (Nat. Astron.), Lusso et al. 2019 (A&A 628 L4), Bargiacchi et al. 2021 fit a truncated log-polynomial (or Taylor/y) expansion of d_L to quasar + SN + GRB data up to z ≈ 7 and report a > 4σ deviation of the fitted coefficients from the values ΛCDM implies. Critics (Yang, Banerjee, Ó Colgáin 2020, 1911.01681; Banerjee et al. 2021, 2009.04109) argue, numerically, that exact ΛCDM itself is badly represented by the truncated series at z ≳ 2, so the deviation is expected. The mathematical half of this dispute is decidable by proof.
- **Intended checkable result:** Lean theorems, for flat ΛCDM over an interval of Ω_m (e.g. [0.2, 0.4]): (i) the coefficients a₂, a₃, a₄ implied by matching at z = 0 (already screened: correct in all papers); (ii) rigorous lower bounds on |μ_ΛCDM(z) − μ_truncated(z)| (distance modulus) on the data's redshift range, compared with the reported error budget; (iii) ideally: no choice of the truncated coefficients near the ΛCDM-implied values reproduces exact ΛCDM within the data errors on [z₁, z₂]. Statement: "if ΛCDM is true, a fit with this truncation must find coefficients ≥ k σ away from the Taylor values" — a settled question (36.4, highest kind) under explicit assumptions.
- **Who benefits:** the quasar/GRB Hubble-diagram community and everyone citing the tension; cosmography as a method (when a truncated series is legitimate at all).
- **Why present methods are insufficient:** both sides argue with numerics and mock fits; nobody has a proof of the truncation error over the relevant range, and the published convergence discussions (Cattoën–Visser 2007) are about radius of convergence, not error bounds in the fitted variable.
- **Strongest current evidence:** Yang et al. Table 2 (Ω_m inferred from a_n inconsistent; a₄ needs Ω_m > 1); Banerjee et al. mock failures (8σ/6σ). Screen 1628Z: the coefficient algebra on both sides is correct, so the dispute is about truncation, not misprints.
- **Falsifier / proof obligation:** if the proven truncation error over the data range is below the data errors for all Ω_m in range, the critique fails and the tension claim stands mathematically; if above, the tension test as performed cannot distinguish ΛCDM.
- **First decisive bottleneck:** rigorous enclosures of the ΛCDM integral χ(z) = ∫₀^z dz′/√(Ω(1+z′)³ + 1 − Ω) at finitely many z in Lean (monotone bounds / Riemann sums with error terms, or a certified series with remainder), uniformly in Ω on an interval. Then the exact log-polynomial value and the subtraction are algebra.
- **Ethics/misuse:** none.
- **First milestone (≤ 3 runs):** Lean theorem: for Ω_m ∈ [0.25, 0.35], the third-order log-polynomial with ΛCDM-implied a₂, a₃ misses the exact ΛCDM distance modulus by more than 0.1 mag at z = 5 (or whatever the certified number is; frozen before proof with the paper's exact definitions).
