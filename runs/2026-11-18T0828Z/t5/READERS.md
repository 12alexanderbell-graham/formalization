**Verdict by the pre-registered rule: not effective** (3/3 misled). Every K reader saw and quoted the generated "none" next to W′ and resolved the conflict the same way: the prose is read as *later* than the generated record ("later found flawed"), so a generated line that contradicts it is taken as not yet updated, not as evidence against it. Three generated lines in a row — kernel status (0228Z), fidelity record (H), objection registry (K) — moved "no" to "unclear" and no further: 9/9 readers withheld citation.

**What this measures.** The asymmetry is in the reader, and it is a reasonable one: a specific, dated, negative claim by the authors is treated as the most recent and most cautious signal, and no generated line can show that it is *older* than the claim, because the claim is never recorded anywhere the generator reads. The findings-page result (contradicted lines mislead 0/3) held for claims about *content* (a range, a number) that the generated text states; a status or objection line states the absence of something, which a reader reads as "not yet". So the defence against W′ is not on the page a reader sees but upstream: a hand-written sentence that withdraws or objects to a listed headline must not exist unless the objection is registered (and then the generated page shows it, which is correct). The registry stays (it is where a real objection must now appear, and it is printed on both pages); the next part-3 step is the rule-1 consequence of the brief: a blocking check that every line of `STATE.md`, `README.md` and the run records that names a listed headline in a withdrawal or objection sentence has a registry entry. Run 0228Z's S3 adversary (≈ 40 bypasses of a word rule) is the known obstacle; the check therefore needs the opposite default — a line that names a listed headline outside the generated status forms is flagged unless it matches an allow-list of non-status shapes — and its own blind red team.
