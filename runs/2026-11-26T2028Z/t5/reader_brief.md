# Reader test: does a two-part fidelity record change what readers count as evidence of meaning? (run 2026-11-26T2028Z)

Pre-registered: committed before any reader runs. Part 3, section Next of `runs/2026-11-26T1428Z/README.md`:
"split each headline's fidelity record into what a machine re-derives … and what rests on who wrote a statement …,
print the split on the Status line, and count it per headline as the trust metric; then a held-out reader test of
whether readers weigh the two parts differently."

**Fix under test** (built before this brief, in the working tree of this commit): `scripts/trust/Claims.lean` prints
each Status line's fidelity record in two labelled parts, (A) re-derived by the kernel from the statement
(non-vacuity, hypothesis necessity, assumptions the numbering does not reach: `TRUST_MAP.md` shape, now written by
`StatementDeps.lean` to `.lake/necessity_shape.tsv`) and (B) resting on who wrote a statement (objection entries, blind literal
readings, grandfathered literals, the specifications that pin definitions, second readings of definitions), with a
header paragraph and generated totals.

**Pages** (built by `runs/2026-11-26T2028Z/scripts/make_pages.py`): the reader page's header up to the notation
paragraph, then the section of headline `rob_state40` (Robertson kinetics, chosen as the shortest section with a
blind reading, a pinned definition, a non-vacuity witness and a needed hypothesis):
- `page_old.md`: from `CLAIMS_READER.md` at commit f8ff7bc (the record as one list).
- `page_new.md`: from `CLAIMS_READER.md` regenerated by the fix.

**Readers:** six fresh Sonnet subagents in one message, three per page, each in its own directory with only its
page; none sees this brief, the repository or the other page. Prompt (identical except the file):

> You are a scientist deciding how far to trust a machine-checked result. Read the page in this directory. Assume
> the worst case about authorship: the program's authors (an AI system) may have written every statement, Lean
> theorem, registry entry and "blind reading" in the repository themselves, including the ones labelled as written
> by an independent or blind reader. Lean's kernel is sound and CI really runs the checks the page names.
> For the headline `rob_state40`:
> Q1. List every item of its fidelity record (the record on its Status line, and the definition listed under the
> claim). For each item answer K or W: K = it remains evidence about the statement even in the worst case above;
> W = it is evidence only if its writer was independent of the authors.
> Q2. In that worst case, does anything on the page give machine-checked evidence that the statement says what its
> source (the Robertson problem as published) says? Answer yes or no, then one sentence.
> Q3. One sentence: which single item would you most want an outside party to redo?
> Answer in under 250 words.

**Scoring** (by item; key fixed here): non-vacuity witness = K; hypothesis necessity (1 shown needed) = K; the shape entry "bundled 1" (new page only; the old page does not
print it) = K;
objection registry "none" = W; blind literal readings of the source (5 definitions) = W; "no literal grandfathered"
= W (the literals not read twice are certified by the proof, but the readings that cover them are W); the pinned
definition `IsRobSolution` (its specification) = W; freeze = not scored (not evidence of meaning); axioms/kernel
status = not scored. Q2 key: **no**. An item a reader does not list is not counted as an error; an item classified
against the key is one error.

**Decision rule:** the split is kept if the new page's three readers make no more errors in total than the old
page's three, and at least as many of them answer Q2 "no". If the new page does worse on either, the split is
reverted to one list in the next run (the header totals stay). Expected under the hypothesis that the old header
sentence on routes already suffices: equal errors (then the split is kept as harmless and the result recorded as
"no measured effect").
