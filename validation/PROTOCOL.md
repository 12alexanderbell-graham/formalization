# Expert validation protocol (THE RESULT, part 4)

Version 1, written 2026-10-31 (run 0828Z), before any expert is contacted. Pre-registered: the
SHA-256 of this file seeds the draw of the sample (`validation/make_packet.py`), so any edit to
the protocol redraws the sample and is visible in git history. Changes after the packet is sent
are not allowed; a new version means a new round.

## 1. Question

Does the system certify anything that an independent expert, checking by hand, finds wrong?
The claim to be validated (TARGETS.md, THE RESULT part 4): *on a random sample of certified
outputs, independent experts find no material error that the system did not already flag.*
This is done once, to validate the system as a whole; later outputs inherit the validation
only to the extent that they pass the same mechanical checks.

## 2. What is checked

A **certified output** is a registered headline theorem (`scripts/trust/headlines.txt`) as the
system presents it to a scientist: its plain-words claim (the Lean docstring), its formal
statement, the definitions it uses (verbatim), the source it is about, and the list of
mechanical checks it passed (row of the generated `TRUST_MAP.md`). Each packet item is exactly
that, generated by script; nothing in a genuine item is written for the packet.

Experts answer four questions per item (`packet/ANSWER_FORM.md`): Q1 is the plain claim true;
Q2 does the formal statement say what the plain claim says (hypotheses, quantifiers, definitions,
vacuity, junk values); Q3 is the source represented correctly; Q4 is every number right.

## 3. Sample

- Population: every registered headline at the packet commit (125 at version 1), grouped as in
  `headlines.txt` (F0–F11, T1 fidelity pins, certified numbers, B1, chemistry).
- 40 genuine items: one per group, the rest allocated in proportion to group size (largest
  remainder, ties broken by the seeded generator), drawn without replacement within groups.
- 8 planted control items (`controls.json`): copies of other headlines with one known material
  defect each, one per class the system is meant to stop: provenance, wrong number in the
  statement, statement contradicting the claim, overstated scope, wrong constant, vacuous
  statement, wrong definition, relabelled number. Their base headlines are excluded from the
  draw. Positions are shuffled by the same seed. Experts are told that planted items exist but
  not how many or which.
- The answer key (`sealed/KEY.json`) is committed with the packet and its SHA-256 printed in
  `packet/MANIFEST.md`. Experts receive only `packet/` (as files or PDF), not the repository.
  **The repository must not be shown to experts until their answer forms are in**: if it is
  public at that time, the planted items can be found by searching it, and the round measures
  nothing about planted items.

## 4. Experts and procedure (Alexander contacts; the program never does, annex 47.1)

- At least three reviewers, independent of the program and of each other: one cosmologist
  (items F0–F11, B1, certified numbers, T1 pins), one chemical thermodynamicist (chemistry items),
  one person who reads Lean 4 and Mathlib (Q2 on every item). Every item is read by at least two
  reviewers (its domain reviewer and the Lean reader).
- Each reviewer receives `packet/` and the cited source papers, works alone, may use pen and
  paper, a CAS and the cited papers, and must not consult the repository, the program's records
  or each other before submitting. Time is recorded per item.
- Reviewers return `ANSWER_FORM.md`. Alexander commits the forms unchanged to
  `validation/responses/<reviewer>/` (initials or a code; no personal data needed).

## 5. Scoring (fixed now)

- **Reported defect:** any NO answer with a description.
- **Adjudication (by the program, recorded in `validation/ADJUDICATION.md`):** each reported
  defect on a genuine item is classified within one run as
  (a) *refuted*: the program shows by a Lean proof or an exact computation that the item is right
  as stated; the reviewer is shown the argument and may contest it; a contested refutation counts
  as a miss;
  (b) *already flagged*: the defect is stated in the item itself (claim, statement, or listed
  checks). Being recorded elsewhere in the repository (STATE "Known gaps", FINDINGS, audit
  notes) but not in the item does **not** count as flagged: a scientist reading the item would
  not see it;
  (c) *minor*: wording that misleads nobody, agreed by the reviewer;
  (d) otherwise a **miss**.
  The burden of proof is on the system: anything not refuted, flagged or agreed minor is a miss.
- **Reviewer sensitivity:** a planted item is *caught* when a reviewer reports a NO that names its
  defect (or an equivalent description). Sensitivity = caught planted items / planted items, per
  reviewer and pooled (an item caught by any of its reviewers counts as caught for the pool).
- **Validity of the round:** the round counts only if pooled sensitivity ≥ 7/8 and every reviewer
  catches at least 3/4 of the planted items they read. Otherwise the round is reported as uninformative and repeated with
  other reviewers; misses found in an invalid round still count.
- **Result:** number of misses among the 40 genuine items. Zero misses gives a one-sided 95%
  upper bound on the per-item material-miss rate of 1 − 0.05^{1/40} = 7.2% (the bound tightens
  only with more items; a second round of 40 fresh items brings it to 3.7%). Report the bound,
  never "no errors".

## 6. What follows from the result

- Every miss is an integrity event (LEDGER): the item is corrected or demoted, and the class of
  error gets a mechanical check that would have caught it, which is then measured on the T5
  benchmark (THE RESULT part 3). The round is repeated after the fix with fresh items.
- Planted items caught by reviewers but which a T5 judge run would have *accepted* are recorded
  as gaps of the mechanical pipeline (four planted classes are T5 open holes: overstated scope,
  wrong definition, relabelled number, provenance).
- The protocol, packet, key, responses and adjudication are published with the paper.

## 7. Known weaknesses of this design (stated before the round)

- 40 items bound the miss rate at 7.2%, not at zero; "the system missed nothing" is a sample
  statement.
- The plain-words claims are the program's own Lean docstrings; some are terse and cite
  equation numbers without the paper, which the item's Source line supplies from FINDINGS.md.
  Reviewers may report terseness as MINOR; it is not a miss unless it misleads.
- Items are headlines; helper lemmas and the proofs themselves are trusted to the Lean kernel and
  are not reviewed.
- Several headlines are close siblings (e.g. two-sided and past-only versions of a refutation);
  the sample may contain both. The allocation is per group, not per finding.
